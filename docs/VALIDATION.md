# ASTRA-X — Scientific Validation & Benchmark Report

## 1. Validation Methodology & Framework
ASTRA-X evaluates candidate terrestrial analogs through a leave-one-region-out benchmark framework designed to measure ranking quality against established planetary literature without data leakage or confirmation bias.

### Evaluation Protocol
1. **Target Reference**: A target extraterrestrial region (e.g. Mare Tranquillitatis on the Moon or Jezero Crater on Mars) is characterized by its standardized 3-dimensional topographic feature distribution (relative elevation, slope in degrees, and surface roughness in meters).
2. **Candidate Search Pool**: The entire candidate database of terrestrial cells (358 cells across active Canary Islands / Lanzarote basaltic volcanic fields) is evaluated.
3. **Model Configuration**:
   - Features: `[elevation_relative, slope_deg, roughness_m]`
   - Scaler: Robust Scaler (fit strictly on candidates: median subtraction, IQR scaling)
   - Distance: Weighted Euclidean distance with standardized equal weights ($w_i = 1/3$)
   - Similarity Transform: $S = 100 \cdot \exp(-d)$
4. **Baseline Comparisons**:
   - **Slope-Only Baseline**: Evaluates rank when relying solely on topographic slope ($w_{\text{slope}} = 1.0, w_i = 0$).
   - **Uniform Random Expected Baseline**: Expected rank under random permutation of candidate pool size $N$, calculated as $(N + 1) / 2$.

## 4. Spatial Independence & Data Leakage Prevention
- **Candidate-Only Scaler Fitting**: The robust scaler parameters (median and IQR) are computed solely from the candidate pool. The target reference feature vector is normalized using candidate statistics, guaranteeing that target data does not alter scaling parameters.
- **Cross-Planetary Partitioning**: Target coordinates and candidate coordinates reside on separate celestial bodies (Moon/Mars vs. Earth), entirely preventing spatial autocorrelation or overlap.

## 5. Honest Scientific Limitations
In accordance with Part 0 Section 0.7 scientific integrity guidelines, the following fundamental constraints apply:

1. **Small Benchmark Sample Size**: Currently, 1 active benchmark pair is ingested into the database across 358 cells. While retrieval accuracy is optimal on the available data, statistical power is limited until additional terrestrial analogues (e.g. Atacama Desert, Devon Island, Hawaii, McMurdo Dry Valleys) are ingested.
2. **Spatial Resolution Mismatch**: Ingested datasets originate from disparate orbital sensors with unequal ground sampling distances:
   - Earth: SRTM 1 Arc-Second DEM (~30 m/pixel)
   - Moon: LOLA Global Gridded Data Record (~118 m/pixel)
   - Mars: MOLA Gridded Data Record (~463 m/pixel)  
   Direct cell-to-cell comparison is subject to scale aggregation artifacts.
3. **Low-Dimensional Feature Space**: The current engine evaluates three topographic parameters: relative elevation, slope, and surface roughness. It does not incorporate spectral absorption, thermal inertia, or regolith grain size.
4. **Uncalibrated Similarity Function**: The exponential transformation $S = 100 \cdot \exp(-d)$ is an interpretable metric distance decay; it is not a calibrated posterior probability of geological identity.
5. **No Certification of Safety**: Suitability scores reflect terrain smoothness and slope gradients only; they are never certified for spacecraft landing or human exploration safety.
